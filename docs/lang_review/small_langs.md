# Manx, Cornish, Jèrriais, Guernésiais: review notes (wave 45, session F)

Two lessons each, on your steer. Each file also gains `name`, `endonym` and `about`. Existing places, greetings and
phrases are kept as they were. The `say` guides are rough approximations and would be worth a speaker's check.

- Manx (gv): Gaiety Theatre and the kipper shop, Douglas. Ned Maddrell died 1974; Bunscoill Ghaelgagh, St John's.
  "Traa dy liooar" is in the OED. The numbers (nane, jees, tree, kiare, queig, shey, shiaght, hoght, nuy, jeih) were
  left out to keep it to two lessons.
- Cornish (kw): Admiral Benbow and Chapel Street Books, Penzance. Dolly Pentreath d. 1777; UNESCO reclassified it in
  2010. Spellings are SWF as I know it. Question: SWF "onan" or "onen" for one, if a numbers lesson is ever wanted.
- Jèrriais (jer): Crapaud Arms and Central Market, St Helier. Phrases and the 2001 census figure (2,874, about 3%)
  come from gov.je's language page. Question: "Jersey has toads, Guernsey has none" is island lore; fine to keep?
- Guernésiais (nrf_gg): Fish Quay Café and Gâche Bakery, St Peter Port. Phrases follow the forms on Wikipedia's
  Guernésiais page; the census figure is 2001 (1,327 fluent). Question: the formal "Baon-n-jour" versus the
  "Bonjour" already in the file. And is "âne" (the Guernsey donkey) wanted as a word?
