# Scottish Gaelic (gd): questions for the owner (wave 45, session E)

Six short lessons in data/lang/gd.json (gd_01 to gd_06, levels 1 to 4), teachers in Stornoway, Harris, Portree,
Tobermory and the Braes. Everything in the game is a form I'm confident of. These are worth a fluent speaker's eye:

1. **Pronunciation guides** are rough English-spelling approximations (no IPA). Gaelic sounds such as the broad and
   slender consonants don't map neatly, e.g. `a h-aon` given as "uh HOON", `ceann` as "KYOWN", `àird` as "AHRJ".
   Happy to tune any a speaker winces at.
2. **About line, the 2022 census**: "about 130,000 people in Scotland with some Gaelic". I'm fairly sure of the
   130,161 figure for people aged 3+ with some Gaelic skills; worth checking against Scotland's Census before release.
3. **"is" for "and"** (tì is aran, the cafe sign "Tì, aran is càise"): used as the short form of agus. Fine in
   speech and signs, but a speaker might prefer "agus" on a printed sign.
4. **Loanword notes**: whisky from uisge-beatha and slogan from sluagh-ghairm are standard etymologies; galore is
   given as from gu leòr (Gaelic) and go leor (Irish), since dictionaries credit both.
5. Left out on purpose: dialect differences between Lewis, Harris, Skye and Argyll; place-name examples I wasn't sure
   of (e.g. Bal- names, several of which come from bealach, a pass, not baile).
6. Not mine, spotted in passing: data/zones/harris_blackhouse.json is named "Gearrannan Blackhouse", but Gearrannan
   is on Lewis (Carloway), not Harris. The zone's own plaque already says Lewis, so it may be deliberate (the Harris
   zone covers both). No change made.
