# Animal names in Welsh, Irish and Scottish Gaelic: review list

Written for wave 45 session R (Fieldbook teaching). Every creature in `data/creatures/*.json` now has a `names` field
with the real basis's name in Welsh (`cy`), Irish (`ga`) and Scottish Gaelic (`gd`). A language is left out unless the
word is certain. This file lists, for the owner to check: the gaps, the creatures based on something other than one real
animal, and the names that are a general word ("seal", "crab") rather than the exact species.

Please check before shipping (owner is Welsh; a Gaeilge and a Gàidhlig speaker should look at the ga and gd columns).

## 4. Words worth a second look

- Kelpie: `ga each uisce` / `gd each-uisge` are the water horse, a close cousin rather than the kelpie itself.
- Merrow: `cy môr-forwyn` and `gd maighdeann-mhara` are the general words for a mermaid; `ga murúch` is the merrow.
- Natterjack: `ga cnádán` given; Welsh and Gaelic left out.
- Moldiwarp, Tunneller, Tubemole: `cy gwadd / twrch daear`; both are in use, north and south.
- Orca: `ga cráin dhubh` given; Welsh and Gaelic left out.
- Morrígan: Irish spelling left out because modern forms vary (Mórrígan, Mór-Ríon).
- Stoat: `gd` left out because sources differ on whether `neas` is the stoat or the weasel.
