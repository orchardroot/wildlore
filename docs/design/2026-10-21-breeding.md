# Good Stock: a breeding and genetics design

*2026-10-21. A proposal for breeding in Wildlore: These Isles, written against the code as it stands (file names in brackets are the systems each idea extends). Companion to 2026-10-21-deeper-wildlore.md, whose identity line applies: a walking holiday round the real Isles that turns into a life there.*

The owner's ask: "It should really expand on what Pokémon achieved and really look deeply into the monsters mixing and create hybrids where appropriate or monsters with merged types or skills. This should be educational in terms of explaining genetic inheritance and have enough complexity to be engaging as a feature."

## 1. What Pokémon did, and where it stopped

Pokémon's breeding (since 1999) is a hidden slot machine. Two parents in the same opaque compatibility group produce an egg; one shape-shifting species breeds with anything; the child draws hidden stats partly from its parents, keeps a temperament if a parent holds a stone, learns a few moves a parent knew, and has a small chance of a rare passive. All of it is real design and none of it is visible or explained. The groups are never named in play, the numbers are never shown, the "why" of a 1 in 8 outcome is nowhere. Players reverse-engineered it from the code and then ground thousands of eggs for a perfect one. The parents are inert afterwards, and two different species never produce anything but the mother's.

What it got right, and what Wildlore keeps: breeding makes you care about an individual rather than a species, pairing feels like husbandry, and a hatchling you raised is yours in a way a catch is not.

What Wildlore does differently, because of what the game is:

- **Visible and inferable.** The genome is a small, named thing you can look at, and most of what you learn about it you learn by crossing and watching, which is how Mendel learnt it. Carriers are found by test cross, as breeders did before anyone had the word "gene".
- **Real places, real science, real history.** A rare breeds farm in the Cotswolds, a research institute outside Edinburgh, plaques about the people who worked it out, and a conservation thread (the wildcat, the red deer, the Kerry cow) that is true and current.
- **Hybrids only where nature or folklore allows.** A mule is real and sterile; a hare cannot cross with a rabbit; a wildcat crossed with a moggy is the saddest fact in Scottish natural history. The game says which is which.
- **Belonging, not power.** You breed to bring a line back, to win a rosette at the Cheshire Show, to own a creature nobody else has, and to understand what you are looking at. Nothing bred outclasses the story, and none of it is needed for the credits.
- **Wry and 16+.** The farmer has opinions, the creatures go behind the barn, the notebook is honest about what it does not know. Welfare is taken seriously, which mostly means the farmer refusing things rather than the game depicting them.

## 2. The genetics model

Real enough that a biology teacher would not wince; small enough that a player who never opens the genome view still gets a lovely hatchling. Every gene maps to something you can see or feel. Every creature already in a save gets a genome without changing anything the player has seen.

Each creature carries a `genome`: a dictionary of loci, each holding two alleles, one from each parent. The vocabulary is the game's own: a creature's genome is its **makings**, the pair at a locus is its **hand**, a visible outcome is its **showing**. Seven loci, each chosen to teach one idea.

| Locus | Alleles | Rule taught | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coat** | `+` usual (dominant), `c` coloured (recessive) | Dominance; carriers | `cc` wears an uncommon coat (the existing `Creature.variant`); `+c` looks ordinary and carries |
| **Tint** | `W` winter, `R` russet, `G` gold, `S` spring, `D` storm, `M` moonlit | Co-dominance; and epistasis, since Tint is only read when Coat is `cc` | Which coat a `cc` creature wears. Two different tints both show, as a **roan** coat (both palettes interleaved, as in roan cattle) |
| **Mark** | `B` bold, `b` faint | Incomplete dominance | `BB` strong markings, `Bb` medium, `bb` none: the sprite recipe's pattern density. Red, pink, white snapdragons |
| **Knack** | `K` the line's knack (dominant), `k` the line's **second knack** (recessive) | A recessive that shows only when both parents carry it | `kk` has the line's hidden second passive (a new `second` column in `data/knacks.json`). One wild creature in ten is `Kk` |
| **Ginger** | `O`, `o`, on the sex chromosome | Sex linkage | A warm hue shift to the palette. In the two-copy sex, `Oo` shows as **tortoiseshell** patches (nearly every tortoiseshell cat is female). In the one-copy sex, what you have is what you show |
| **Frame** (four loci) | `+` or `-` at each | Polygenic traits, the bell curve, regression to the mean | Size, 0 to 8 pluses: sprite scale 0.9 to 1.1 and up to plus or minus 2 on HP and Speed (big is sturdy and slow). Most land in the middle, which is Galton's point |
| **Mark and Knack are linked** | recombination 15% | Linkage; a visible marker for a hidden gene | Bold markings tend to travel with the second knack in wild stock, so a breeder who notices can select on what they can see, as real breeders use coat markers, and as Bateson and Punnett found in sweet peas |

Three rules sit on top:

- **Mutation.** One hatchling in 64 has one allele rewritten at random: a tint neither parent had, a Frame flip, a `k` in a line where nobody carried it. Called a **sport**, the old breeders' word. Random and undirected, the only way a new allele enters a closed line. The Ancon sheep of 1791 (a short-legged ram born on a Massachusetts farm and bred from on purpose) is the plaque.
- **Hybrid vigour.** Unrelated parents give the hatchling inborn values drawn from the parents' average plus 1 and a slightly higher cap. Crossbred cattle and mongrel dogs are the examples.
- **Inbreeding depression.** Kinship is Wright's coefficient over a three-generation pedigree (`uid` of sire and dam, and theirs). At half-sibling kinship and above, values are drawn from the average minus 1, the cap drops, and a hidden recessive **fault** allele at Knack (`f`) shows as a weak stat when doubled. The farmer refuses parent-child and full-sibling pairings outright ("not on my farm"), so the game never depicts the worst of it. Florida panthers and pedigree dogs are the plaques.

Inborn stat values (`Creature.ivs`, 0 to 15, never shown by that name) are the polygenic layer the game already has, and breeding treats them as quantitative genetics does: each stat is the mid-parent value plus a small random spread, capped at the better parent plus 2, with the vigour and kinship adjustments. Selection works, slowly, and regresses to the mean, which is honest. Temperament (`Creature.nature`, shown in the UI today as "nature", to be renamed **temper** in this release) comes from one parent two times in three and is rolled fresh otherwise; the notebook says plainly that temperament is partly born and partly made and the game does not pretend to a gene for it.

**Sex.** Creatures get a `sex`: `m`, `f` or none. Beasts are XY with the male as the one-copy sex; Fowl are ZW with the female as the one-copy sex, because birds really are the other way round (Punnett's auto-sexing hens are the plaque). Spirits, Forged, Legends and fungi have no sex and do not breed; the farmer's line about the fungi is that a split-gill mushroom has over twenty thousand mating types and she is not getting involved.

**Genomes for creatures you already have.** Nothing is rolled. `Genetics.derive(c)` seeds a hash from the creature's inborn values, its temper and its evolution line (the line id, recorded by `Dex` at load from each file's `line` field, so evolving does not change the answer) and draws alleles at wild frequencies: Coat `+c` in 1 of 8 plain creatures and `cc` for anything already wearing a coat, with Tint homozygous for that coat; Knack `Kk` in 1 of 10; Frame as four fair coins; sex from the same hash. A 1.0 creature therefore has a fixed genome the first time anyone asks, written into the save at the next save so it can never drift. Everything is a `from_dict` default plus derive-if-missing; no save version bump.

## 3. Compatibility and hybrids

**Kindreds.** Every evolution line gets a `kindred` in `data/genes.json` (validator-checked): **Beast** (mammals, including seals and whales), **Fowl**, **Scale** (reptiles and amphibians), **Fin**, **Shell**, **Wing** (insects), **Green** (plants and fungi: no breeding), **Uncanny** (fae and spirits: no breeding, one folklore exception), **Forged** (iron and urban machines: no), **Legend** (no, there is one of each). Breeding within a line is always allowed. Across lines it is allowed only where `data/hybrids.json` lists the pair; otherwise the farmer explains why not, which is the educational point ("a hare and a rabbit are different genera with different chromosome counts; the jackalope is a postcard").

**Three outcomes of a cross.** Same line: the line's first stage, genome drawn from both parents. **Crossbreed** (one real species, two game lines): Dexter, Highland and Kerry cattle (`moolet`, `coolet`, `kerryspot`) are one species, as are Herdwick and Loaghtan sheep; the hatchling is the dam's line carrying the sire's alleles, fully fertile, and the notebook explains breed versus species. **Hybrid** (two species): a new creature from `data/hybrids.json` with its own entry in the Fieldbook's Hybrids section.

| Cross | Result | Fertile | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wildkit or Highlynx x **Moggle** (new appendix species: the Macclesfield moggy, Urban/Kin, in any town at night) | **Hybrid wildcat**, Night/Kin | Yes | **Real**, and the central conservation problem for the Scottish wildcat: the wild population is now largely hybrid after decades of crossing with domestic cats. Saving Wildcats (RZSS, Cairngorms) has bred and released cats since 2023 and uses genetic scoring. The game's goal is a line that scores pure |
| Stagmor or Fawnlet x Sikawhistle | **Sika-red**, Kin/Gale | Yes | **Real**: red and sika deer hybridise freely; introgression is a live problem in Scotland, Wicklow and Kerry |
| Gealhare x Snowhare (brown x mountain hare) | **Blue-brown hare**, Kin/Frost | Yes | **Real**, documented in Scotland and Sweden, with brown hare genes found in mountain hares. Low odds, as in nature |
| Chipgull x a new **Blackback** (lesser black-backed gull) | **Mongrel gull**, Urban/Tide | Yes | **Real**: herring and lesser black-backed gulls hybridise, and glaucous x herring crosses are common enough to have names. Optional |
| Capercaillie x a new **Blackcock** (black grouse) | **Rackelhane**, Hedgerow/Brawn | No (males sterile) | **Real**, and that is its real name, from Scandinavia; recorded in Scotland. Optional |
| Connie or Foalmoor x a new **Donkey** (the Weston-super-Mare beach donkey, Kin/Hearth) | **Mule** (donkey sire) or **Hinny** (horse sire) | No | **Real**; the odd chromosome count (63) is why. Worth adding the donkey for: the mule is the clearest sterile hybrid anyone knows |
| Orcalet x Dolphlet | **Wholphin**-style cross, Tide/Frost | Rarely | **Game invention**, nodding to the real wholphin (false killer whale x bottlenose, Hawaii, 1985, which was fertile). Orca x bottlenose is not documented; the entry says so |
| Kelpie x Connie | **Loch foal**, Brook/Kin | No | **Folklore**: Highland tales have the water-horse siring foals on mares. Flag: I am not certain this is in the recorded tales rather than later retellings; keep it as "they say" |
| Selkie x Greyhaul | **Selkie pup**, Tide/Fae | Yes | **Folklore**, the one Uncanny exception; the tales are explicit that selkies have children |
| Pooka x Gealhare | **Puck hare**, Fae/Kin | No | **Game invention** with a folklore excuse (the pooka takes hare form) |
| Bodminax x Moggle | **Beast kitten**, Night/Urban | No | **Game invention**; since the 1995 government report found no evidence of the beast, the entry is written as a rumour |

Refused, with the reason given in play: hare x rabbit (genera, chromosome counts 48 and 44); stoat, pine marten, mink and badger with each other (all mustelids, all different genera, no viable crosses); sheep x goat (a handful of live cases on record, nearly all stillborn; Dot will not try); any Beast x Fowl; anything x a Legend.

**Hybrid rules.**
- *Types:* the sire's first type and the dam's first type, or the shared type plus an authored secondary. Authored in the data, never computed, so every hybrid is designed.
- *Stats:* the average of the parents' first stages plus 5% on the two stats both parents are best at, capped so the total never beats the better parent's final stage. `growth` and `base_xp` from the dam; `catch_rate` irrelevant.
- *Moves:* an authored learnset drawn from both parents at the same levels, keeping both types' moves, plus **heirloom tricks** (section 4).
- *Knack:* the parent whose `K` the hybrid got; `kk` gives the sire's second knack. Hybrids carry a full genome.
- *Sterility:* `fertile: false` means Dot declines the pairing with the mule explanation; the creature is otherwise complete and the fact is in its entry. Fertile hybrids breed back to either parent, which is how introgression works and how the wildcat goal is scored (pure means no Moggle in three generations).
- *Naming:* authored names first (Rackelhane, Mule, Hinny), with a fallback portmanteau of the sire's first syllable and the dam's last, so the convention matches the real one (a liger's father is a lion, a tigon's a tiger), and the notebook says so.
- *Fieldbook:* a third section, **Hybrids**, numbered `H01` onwards after the Appendix, shown once you have bred or seen one (as the Appendix appears once you have met a fungus). Never counted for the diploma, milestones, stamps or Garside's rewards: `Dex.core_count` already excludes the Appendix and gains the same exclusion for `hybrid: true`. The dex order stays clean.

## 4. The loop

**Where.** *Guiting Rare Breeds Farm*, a yard-and-interiors zone off the Cotswolds (the 3.0 Oxford and Cotswolds area), modelled on the Cotswold Farm Park at Guiting Power and the Rare Breeds Survival Trust's work (the Trust from 1973; the Farm Park from 1971, founded by one of the Trust's founders). The name is the game's own so as not to trade on a real business. Run by **Dot Tallis**, a farmer in her sixties with a Gloucestershire accent, a clipboard and no patience for nonsense, who becomes one of 3.5's Regulars. The yard has the stud board, the lambing shed, the incubator room and a wall of plaques for the Soay, the Kerry, the Loaghtan and the Herdwick. *The Roslin Institute* at Easter Bush, south of Edinburgh (real, part of the University; Dolly the sheep was born there on 5 July 1996 and announced in February 1997), is a visitor room and a lab with **Dr Morag Keir**, a geneticist who explains what Dot does by feel: linkage, the Punnett square, kinship, the genotype test. Dolly herself is in the National Museum of Scotland on Chambers Street, and the game sends you there for the plaque, which says carefully that cloning is copying, not breeding, and that Roslin's day job is animal health and genetics. A sister yard in **Kerry** (the Kerry cow is one of Europe's oldest dairy breeds and is rare) opens the Irish goal and takes euros.

**What happens.**
1. *Pairing* at the farm gate: pick two from your team or the Ark. Both must be at least Fond (`Bond` tier two); the pair must be same line, a listed crossbreed or a listed hybrid; opposite sexes; kinship below half-sibling; both fit. Dot explains any refusal in a line.
2. *The preview* (section 6): a Punnett square per locus with what you know, and the odds of what you are chasing.
3. *A day and a walk.* One pairing per farm per in-game day. The result is a **basket** (eggs: Fowl, Scale, Shell, Wing) or a **bundle** (Beasts: "she's in the lambing shed, I'll ring you"), and it comes to you when you have walked it off: 1,500 to 4,000 steps by kindred (`TrainerStats` already counts steps). Out of season (Beasts and Fowl favour spring, Scale summer, the rest year-round, from `Calendar`) it takes twice the steps and a heat-lamp fee. Season is a modifier, never a wall.
4. *Hatching* anywhere but a battle: a short scene, the hatchling at level 1 (5 for hybrids, which are big babies), its genome revealed locus by locus as far as the notebook knows, a line from a parent if it is in the party, and into the party or the Ark as a catch would be.
5. *Heirloom tricks:* up to two moves from the line's `heirloom` list that either parent knew. The notebook is clear that tricks are taught, not inherited, which is both true and the only sane way to explain egg moves.
6. *Register it* in the Stud Book (section 5). Three baskets carried at once.

**What you are chasing.** A coat you cannot catch (roan and tortoiseshell exist only by breeding; a moonlit-storm roan is a screenshot). A perfect lean (`BB`, Frame 8, `kk`, values at the cap, over four generations: slow, legible, and never better than a strong catch by more than the vigour lift). A hybrid (twelve designed creatures nobody can catch). **Conservation**: three Watchlist goals, each a quest line with Dot and Dr Keir: a pure-scoring wildcat line, a pure red deer line, and the Kerry herd (four Kerryspots with distinct grandparents lent to the Kerry yard). Each pays once: a Trust grant scaled to badges, a plaque, a Regular's favour and a stage for that nation's rewilding site, so the thing you bred goes back to the land. **Rosettes**: the County Show gains a **Breed Class** rule of the day (`Tournament` already rotates rules), judged on coat, frame, markings and pedigree, no battle, at the show's existing prize scale.

**Economy.** Stud fees are the sink: Dot keeps stud stock of each kindred so a lone creature can breed, at a fee keyed on badges like `Jobs.money_for`. Your own creature can stand at stud for an NPC fee once a real day at the same scale, only after a Breed Class win, and that is the only faucet. Hatchlings cannot be sold; the game does not sell creatures and Dot would not have it. A **boarding paddock** is a small Deed under 3.5's `core/deeds.gd` (six breeding stock outside the Ark, with rates and events: a fox, a visiting judge, a heat wave), on the Allotment's pattern. The **genotype test** at Roslin is the other sink: a fee reveals a creature's full hand without a test cross, dear early, cheap late, never required. Not a money printer: `MarketSim` gains a `stud_farm` strategy (breed daily, show weekly, stand at stud) and the test asserts it earns under 1.5 times the main line's prize money per hour before the Moot and never compounds; grants pay once and rosettes already have a daily cap.

## 5. Teaching without lecturing

- **The Stud Book**, a Fieldbook tab beside Rumours and the Gazetteer (`ui/rumours_tab.gd` is the pattern), fills in from what you have seen. Each locus appears at its first showing; each cross you make is logged with expected and observed counts ("Coat, `+c` x `+c`: expected 1 in 4 coloured, seen 2 of 6"), which is the sampling lesson told by your own numbers. A page per idea unlocks when observed: Dominance, Carriers, Co-dominance, Incomplete dominance, Sex linkage, Polygenic, Linkage, Mutation, Vigour and kinship, Breed versus species, Hybrids and sterility, Selection, Conservation. Four short paragraphs, a worked square and one real story each.
- **The Punnett preview** is the instrument, used every time you pair. Unknown alleles show as `?` and the square shows the range of outcomes consistent with what you know, which is what motivates the test cross: breed it with a known `cc` and the hatchlings tell you what it carries. Players will arrive at the method themselves.
- **Dot** teaches by refusing and by noticing ("Bold stripes on that one. Nine times in ten it's carrying the other knack. Don't ask me why, ask the lab."). **Dr Keir** gives the why, once, when asked.
- **Plaques** (Gazetteer, prefix `breed_`), real and checked: Mendel and his peas (Brno, 1866, ignored until 1900); William Bateson at Cambridge coining "genetics" (in a letter in 1905, publicly in 1906); Reginald Punnett, his square, and his auto-sexing chickens that used sex-linked barring so a chick's sex could be read from its down; Edith Rebecca Saunders, Bateson's collaborator; Bateson, Saunders and Punnett finding linkage in sweet peas; Rosalind Franklin at King's College London and Photo 51 (taken in 1952 with her student Raymond Gosling); Crick and Watson at the Cavendish in 1953 (the Eagle pub story told as the story it is); Dolly, Wilmut and Campbell; the Soay sheep of St Kilda, a primitive breed studied on Hirta since 1985 as one of the best-known wild population genetics projects; Kerry cattle; the Loaghtan's four or six horns; the Ancon sheep; the wildcat and Saving Wildcats. Uncertain details are omitted rather than guessed, per the wave rule. Flagged for a check before shipping: the year of the main wildcat hybridisation paper (I believe 2023, in Current Biology) and the date of the first Saving Wildcats releases (I believe June 2023).

## 7. Data model and code plan

New files, pure logic unless stated:
- `core/genetics.gd` (`Genetics`): `LOCI`; `derive(c) -> Dictionary`; `cross(sire, dam, rng) -> Dictionary` (genome, sex, ivs, temper, heirlooms, sport flag); `showing(c) -> Dictionary` (variant string, mark level, frame, knack id, hue shift); `kinship(a, b) -> float`; `punnett(sire, dam, locus, known_only) -> Array`; `can_pair(sire, dam, dex) -> {ok, why, kind}`; `hybrid_species(entry, dex) -> Dictionary`.
- `data/genes.json`: loci, alleles, dominance mode, wild frequencies, kindred and sex mode per line, heirloom moves per line, step counts and seasons per kindred.
- `data/hybrids.json`: `{id, sire_lines, dam_lines, name, types, base, growth, learnset, knack, fertile, real: real|folklore|invented, dex: {text, fact}, sprite: {body: dam, face: sire, palette: mix}}`.
- `core/breeding.gd` (`Breeding`): the loop in flags: `breed_basket_<n>` = `{sire_uid, dam_uid, genome, species, steps_due, started_day}`, `breed_paired_<zone>_<day>`, `breed_log_<n>`, `breed_goal_<id>`, `stud_paid_<day>`; `hatch_ready(data)`, `hatch(data, n, dex) -> Creature`. `core/stud_book.gd` for unlocked ideas and tallies.
- `tools/spritegen/blend.gd`: `blend(sire_recipe, dam_recipe, spec, genome) -> recipe`. Body layers from the dam (the largest ellipses by area, as `_body_index` already finds them), limbs, face layers and eyes from the sire with their offsets rescaled from the sire's body box into the dam's, palettes mixed 50/50 by luminance rank, pattern period from Mark, hue shift from Ginger, scale from Frame, and a roan or tortie dither pass. Deterministic.
- `ui/gene_art.gd`: runtime rendering through `preload("res://tools/spritegen/creature.gd")`, which is Image-API only and deterministic so it runs in the game; cached as `ImageTexture` per genome hash as `CoatArt.texture` caches coats; rendered at preview and hatch, never per frame. Roan and tortie are new `variant` strings (`winter+storm`, `tortie`) that `CoatArt.recolour` learns to parse. Flag: confirm `tools/spritegen` is inside the export include filter in `export_presets.cfg`, or move `creature.gd` and `canvas.gd` under `art/`.
- UI: `ui/pairing_screen.gd`, `ui/punnett_view.gd`, `ui/studbook_tab.gd`, a genome page in `summary_screen.gd`, a basket line on the pause card. World: `data/zones/guiting_farm*.json`, `data/zones/roslin*.json`, dialogue for Dot and Dr Keir, `data/quests/breed_*.json`, plaques `breed_*`, the Moggle and Donkey species and recipes.

Changes to existing files, all additive and defaulting:
- `battle/creature.gd`: `genome := {}`, `sex := ""`, `uid := 0`, `pedigree := {}`, `hybrid := ""`; `from_dict` reads each with a default and calls `Genetics.derive` when `genome` is empty; `to_dict` writes them. A `uid` is assigned on first need from `rng_seed` and a counter flag, so identical twins never collide.
- `data_api/dex.gd`: record `line` per species at `_add`; load `data/hybrids.json` species with `hybrid: true`; `core_count` and `appendix_ids` exclude them; `hybrid_ids()`.
- `core/coats.gd`, `ui/coat_art.gd`: roan and tortie; the Fieldbook coat flags unchanged. `battle/knacks.gd`: `id_for(c)` reads the showing when `kk`, with `Knacks.check` extended. `core/tournament.gd`, `data/shows.json`, `world/show_ring.gd`: the Breed Class and a judge NPC. `ui/pedia_screen.gd`: the Hybrids section. `tools/validate.gd`: `Genetics.check(dex)`: every line has a kindred and a second knack; every hybrid names existing lines in one kindred, has parent-derived types, a sprite spec, a `real` tag and no em-dashes; heirloom moves exist; `genes.json` alleles match the loci table.

Tests (`tests/core/test_genetics.gd` and friends, failing test first as `qol_common.md` asks):
- Ratios over 20,000 seeded crosses: `+c` x `+c` gives `cc` at 25% plus or minus 1.5; Tint `WD` x `WD` 1:2:1 within 2; Mark three showings 1:2:1; a carrier dam and plain sire give half the sons ginger and no daughters; Mark-Knack recombinants 15% plus or minus 2; sports 1 in 64 plus or minus 20%.
- Derivation is deterministic (same dict, same genome, ten times) and stable across evolution.
- Old save fixtures (1.0 and 3.3 dicts with no genome, sex or uid) load, round-trip, and `core_count` is unchanged.
- A mule cannot pair; a fertile hybrid can breed back; full siblings have kinship 0.25, unrelated stock 0; parent-child is refused.
- A hybrid's stat total never exceeds the better parent's final stage; a level-100 bred creature sits inside the existing ceiling plus the vigour lift.
- The sprite blend is deterministic, non-empty, inside the 96 box, with a non-empty icon.
- `MarketSim` `stud_farm` stays under 1.5 times prize money per hour and bounded over 90 days.
- The Punnett preview with unknowns never claims odds outside the range of the possible squares.

## 8. How to build it

Six cloud sessions, the kit first, the rest in parallel once the contract is published in `docs/briefs/wave36.md` in the wave 34 format (signatures, flag namespaces, shared-file rule, test, time box).

- **A. Genetics core.** "Build `core/genetics.gd`, `data/genes.json`, the Creature fields and derivation, with ratio tests." Owns `battle/creature.gd`. Contract: the `Genetics` signatures and the genome shape `{locus: [a, b]}`. Starts first, and can start now; nothing it touches is hot.
- **B. Hybrids and the Fieldbook.** "Author `data/hybrids.json`, the Moggle and Donkey, `Dex` hybrid loading, the Hybrids section, the `core_count` exclusion and validator checks." Depends on A's genome shape. Contract: the hybrid entry shape and `Dex.hybrid_ids()`.
- **C. Sprites.** "Blend two recipes by genome: `blend.gd`, runtime rendering and caching, roan and tortie coats." May stub `Genetics.showing`. Contract: `GeneArt.texture(c, kind) -> Texture2D`.
- **D. The farm and Roslin.** "Guiting and the Roslin lab as zones with Dot and Dr Keir, the loop in `core/breeding.gd`, baskets, steps, seasons, three Watchlist quests, plaques." Depends on A's `can_pair` and `cross`. Owns the `breed_*` flags.
- **E. UI and the Stud Book.** "Pairing screen, Punnett preview, genome page, basket line, Stud Book tab." Depends on A's `punnett` and `showing` and D's flag names. Owns `core/stud_book.gd`.

## 9. Risks

- **Complexity for casual players.** Layers unlock in the order a beginner meets them: a first pairing needs only the farm gate and a Fond pair; the preview shows one locus with the rest greyed until the Stud Book has that page; hybrids and the lab sit behind the second Watchlist quest; kinship appears only when it would refuse something. A player who breeds one hedgehog for a laugh gets a hedgehog and a nice scene and never sees a square.
- **Balance.** Bred creatures sit under the existing ceilings plus a small vigour lift; hybrids are capped by their better parent; no bred creature has a move or knack a caught one cannot, except the twelve hybrids, each tuned to a mid-line final stage. Keepers scale by par already. The `MarketSim` strategy and the ceiling test are the guard.
- **Grind.** One pairing per farm per day, steps rather than time, three baskets, and the genotype test as a paid shortcut: the slow path to any goal is a few hours of ordinary play, the fast path costs money the economy wants spent anyway. Nothing is 1 in 4,096.
- **Performance.** Genetics is a few dictionary reads. Blending renders one 96 by 96 image through the existing software canvas, a few milliseconds at preview and hatch, cached by genome hash. The 2013 iMac does not notice.
- **Save safety.** Every new Creature field has a `from_dict` default; the genome derives deterministically when missing; everything else is flags; no version bump; a 1.0 save loads with no baskets and an unchanged Fieldbook count. A save written by 3.6 and opened in 3.5 loses the genome fields silently and the derivation rebuilds them identically, so even that direction is safe for everything but hybrids, which 3.5 cannot resolve; the usual "saves carry forward" line covers it.
- **IP.** None of Pokémon's nouns in anything the player sees: makings, hand, showing, kindred, basket, bundle, heirloom trick, second knack, Stud Book, Watchlist, temper. The internal `ivs` and `nature` fields stay for save compatibility, and "nature" leaves the summary screen for "temper" in this release.
- **Welfare and taste.** Creatures choose each other (Fond or better, and they go behind the barn off screen), the farmer refuses close kin and sterile pairings rather than the game showing consequences, nothing is culled, sold or bred to fight, hatchlings cannot be sold, the wildcat and deer goals are framed as conservation with real organisations named only in plaques, and the 16+ licence is spent on Dot's turn of phrase and nothing else. Anything about real animal welfare is said once, plainly, in the Stud Book, and the Trust's actual work is described accurately or not at all.
- **Facts.** Sections 3 and 5 are from memory and mostly standard, but each session should check its plaques and its rows of the hybrids table against a reference before shipping, with the two flagged dates and the kelpie detail first.

## 10. Crossbreeds (wave 45, session O)

Authored hybrids stay first. When two lines have no entry in `data/hybrids.json` but are close enough kin, the egg hatches a **crossbreed** generated from the pair (`core/crossbreeds.gd`). Its species id is `x_<sire line>_<dam line>`, sire first as in real hybrid names (a liger's sire is a lion), and `Dex.get_species` derives it on first ask: nothing is stored but the creature itself, so a save holds a plain species id and a 3.6 save is untouched.

- **Closeness** comes from `data/taxa.json`, the real animal behind each breeding line (species, genus, family). Kindred alone could not do it honestly: the dipper is filed under Fin and the red squirrel under Scale. Same species (two breeds: Dexter and Highland, Herdwick and Loaghtan, the two pony and two dolphin lines, the two capercaillie lines) always takes and is fertile, a crossbred rather than a hybrid. Same genus takes half the time and is sterile unless the genus is marked fertile (Felis, Cervus, Lepus yes, Equus no; Sciurus never crosses at all). Same family takes only for the genus pairs listed (Capra and Ovis, the geep), 15% of the time, sterile. Further apart: never, and Dot says why (hare and rabbit, 48 chromosomes against 44; the mustelids). Lines with no single real animal (folklore, machines) never cross.
- **Derived:** the name is a portmanteau of the front of the sire's name and the back of the dam's from a small syllable rule set, pronounceability checks and a deny-list, with a plain double-barrelled fallback (tested over every ordered pair of breeding lines); types are the sire's first and the dam's first (max two); base stats are the mid-parent of the two final stages scaled 0.97, 0.92 or 0.88 by tier with the two shared best stats lifted 5%, always under the better parent's total and at most 500 (section 9); the learnset merges both parents' moves in turn (max 16); growth, base XP and knack from the dam; the sprite is `blend.gd` over the two final-stage recipes (dam's body, sire's head), rendered at runtime by `ui/crossbreed_art.gd` and hooked into `CreatureArt` and `AnimSprite`.
- **Fieldbook:** a Crossbreeds section (C01 onwards) lists only the ones you have bred, each with its sire and dam named, its fertility, a fact that teaches its tier, and one shared lesson (why ligers exist and mules can't breed). Never counted for the diploma.
- **Restricting ugly blends:** `taxa.json` `restricted` takes [sire, dam] pairs whose blend reads as mush; they are then refused.
