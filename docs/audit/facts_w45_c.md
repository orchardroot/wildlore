# Wave 45 C (More evolutions): facts for the owner to check

Every Fieldbook fact in the 33 new evolved forms was written from well-documented public knowledge. These are the
ones worth a second pair of eyes, because they carry a date, a name or a hedge:

- Choughtain: chough returned to Cornwall unaided in 2001 (died out there in the 1970s; the older Cragchough entry says 1973).
- Hexhorn: Ned Maddrell, last native speaker of Manx, died 1974; a Manx-language primary school exists.
- Annwnhound: Cŵn Annwn "white with red ears" (from the Mabinogi, Pwyll Pendefig Dyfed). Welsh spelling "Cŵn Annwn" is used in one fact line only.
- Gruagach: milk poured over a flat "gruagach stone" is a Hebridean and Highland custom, documented by folklorists such as Alexander Carmichael. No Gaelic is taught in the entry beyond the creature's name.
- Keenwife: bean sí, "woman of the fairy mound".
- Rockape: Churchill-and-the-apes is phrased as "is said to", because the 1944 order is reported rather than quoted.
- Tuskbark: muntjac "many from Woburn, about a century ago" is hedged on purpose.
- Mouser: Larry, Chief Mouser to the Cabinet Office, since 2011 (still true at the time of writing, 2026 mid-year knowledge).
- Sandtrot: Donkey Sanctuary, Sidmouth, founded 1969.
- Cahowler: cahow believed extinct about 300 years until 1951; artificial burrows with baffles.
- Sleighlord: Cairngorm reindeer herd 1952, Mikel Utsi, a Sámi herder, from Sweden.
- Glenmonarch: Landseer, The Monarch of the Glen, 1851.
- Damlord: "trials in England have found beavers can reduce flooding downstream" (Devon trial, River Otter and the Ealing Cuddington/Exeter research).

## Language-certificate evolutions (depend on session D's flags)
These evolve only once the matching `lang_cert_<code>_<level>` flag exists, so they wait for session D (and F for Cornish):
Choughtain (`lang_cert_kw_1`), Hexhorn (`lang_cert_gv_1`), Annwnhound (`lang_cert_cy_1`), Gruagach (`lang_cert_gd_1`),
Keenwife (`lang_cert_ga_1`). If a code or level is renamed, change `evolves.flag` in the base species' line file.
