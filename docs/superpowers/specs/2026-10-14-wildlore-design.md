# Wildlore: Design Spec

Date: 2026-10-14. Status: approved in conversation, pending written review.

## 1. Intent

A standalone, Pokémon-inspired monster-catching RPG set across a rough reflection of the UK and Ireland, at "Switch-era" presentation quality (HD-2D). Built in Godot 4 with GDScript, shipped for Mac and Windows through GitHub Releases. The player starts in Macclesfield, Cheshire.

Audience: age 11 and up, including adults. Tone is warm, witty and never grim. Dialogue should land for adults without excluding children. The Creaturepedia is quietly educational: every creature carries a real fact about the animal or legend that inspired it.

Success looks like: a polished, complete 20 to 30 hour adventure with 40 towns, 200 creatures and 10 badges that runs at 60fps on the developer's iMac (i5-4670, GTX 775M Kepler, macOS Monterey) and therefore on most Windows PCs.

### Constraints and assumptions

- Single player only. No online play, no trading.
- Gamepad and keyboard, rebindable.
- All art and audio is original (generated by our own pipeline) or CC0/CC-BY with credit tracked in `CREDITS.md`. No paid assets, no AI image services, no copyrighted music.
- Delivered in milestones (section 9). M1 is a playable vertical slice; 200 creatures and 40 towns is the full-game target.
- Distribution: GitHub Releases, built by CI. itch.io may follow once the vertical slice is worth showing. Builds are unsigned for now.

## 2. World and map

The world is a set of hand-assembled zones (towns, routes, dungeons, landmarks) joined by seamless edge transitions, not one open world. A world map screen shows simplified real coastlines with each town roughly where it really is. Routes are compressed: a route takes 3 to 8 minutes to cross.

### Towns (40)

- England (17): Macclesfield (start), Buxton, Manchester, Liverpool, Chester, Keswick, Lancaster, York, Whitby, Newcastle, Sheffield, Nottingham, Birmingham, Bath, Glastonbury, Penzance, London (finale).
- Wales (5): Llangollen, Llanberis, Aberystwyth, St Davids, Cardiff.
- Scotland (7): Edinburgh, Glasgow, Fort William, Inverness, Portree, Aberdeen, Kirkwall.
- Northern Ireland (3): Belfast, Bushmills, Derry.
- Ireland (7): Dublin, Kilkenny, Cork, Dingle, Galway, Sligo, Newgrange.
- Isle of Man (1): Douglas.

### Landmark areas

Peak District, Lake District fells, Sherwood Forest, Stonehenge and Avebury (the Moot), Tintagel, Dartmoor, Hadrian's Wall, Snowdon, the Cairngorms, Loch Ness, the Burren, Cliffs of Moher, the London Underground, plus assorted tin mines, peat bogs and sea caves.

### Journey shape

A loose loop: North West England, Wales, the South, ferry to Ireland, north into Northern Ireland, ferry to Scotland, then down the east coast to the Moot and the London finale.

### Travel

On foot, then a bike after the first badge. Ferries: Holyhead to Dublin, Cairnryan to Belfast, Liverpool to Douglas, Scottish isles. Rail fast travel unlocks per town once its station has been visited. A late-game legend mount gives flight. Field abilities gate areas (section 4).

## 3. Creatures and types

### Types (16)

Kin (everyday), Hedgerow (plants, woodland), Brook (fresh water), Tide (sea, coast), Hearth (fire, forge), Gale (wind, flight), Storm (lightning), Frost (snow, highland winter), Stone (rock, fell, cave), Bog (peat, decay, toxins), Iron (industry, canals, rail), Urban (city scavengers, tricksters), Fae (folk magic), Spirit (barrows, ghosts), Brawn (strength), Night (nocturnal, shadow).

Dual types multiply. The chart lives in `data/types.json`; the validator enforces that every type has at least one weakness and every type except Kin has at least one SE target. Balance tuning is expected during M2 to M4.

### Weather (signature mechanic)

Each zone has a weighted weather table: Drizzle (common), Downpour, Fog, Gale, Frost, Sunshine (rare). Weather modifies battles (for example Downpour boosts Brook and Tide and weakens Hearth; Fog lowers accuracy; Sunshine boosts Hearth and Hedgerow and gives a small party-wide morale bonus). Time of day (dawn, day, dusk, night) and season (cycled by in-game days) change encounter tables.

### Roster (200)

- About 150 native wildlife: mammals, birds, sea life, fish, amphibians, insects, a few fungi and plants. Arranged as 1 to 3 stage evolution lines.
- About 25 guests: introduced species and famous oddities (grey squirrel, Peak District wallaby, London parakeet, mink, sika deer, edible dormouse, signal crayfish, a bin-raiding gull).
- About 25 folklore and fantasy: brownie, will-o'-the-wisp, pooka, kelpie, boggart, selkie and others, plus 10 legendaries: Nessie, Y Ddraig Goch, Black Shuck, Lambton Worm, Herne the Hunter, the Morrígan, Cù-Sìth, Green Man, Bran's Raven, Manannán's Horse.

Three Cheshire starters: Hedgerow (hedgehog), Brook (otter), Hearth (red squirrel with an ember tail).

### Creature data

One JSON file per evolution line under `data/creatures/`. Each creature has: id, name, types (1 or 2), base stats, growth rate, catch rate, abilities, learnset, evolution rule (level, item, weather, time, friendship), habitat zones, Creaturepedia entry (flavour text plus one real-world fact, written for age 11+), sprite id.

### Stats

HP, Attack, Defence, Lore (special attack), Ward (special defence), Speed. Natures (+10%/-10%) and individual variance (0 to 15 per stat). Training is a visible per-stat value raised by battling, capped per creature (simplified EVs).

### Moves

About 250 moves in `data/moves/`. Each: id, name, type, category (physical, special, status), power, accuracy, PP, priority, effect id plus params. Status conditions: Soaked (speed down, Storm weakness), Chilled (may skip turn), Muddled (may hit self), Hexed (damage over time), Stung (poison damage), Asleep.

### Art

Creature sprites are 96x96 pixel art: front, back, 2-frame idle, plus a 32x32 overworld/party icon. Produced by a generator in `tools/spritegen/` (body templates, part libraries, palettes, per-species recipe files), then hand-tuned where needed.

## 4. Battles and core loop

- Turn-based, mostly singles, doubles for set pieces. Party of 6.
- Turn order by Speed, then priority moves.
- Damage: standard level/attack/defence/power formula times type effectiveness, same-type bonus (1.5x), weather, critical hits (1.5x, 1/24 base) and a random 0.85 to 1.0 roll.
- Catching: weaken, then throw a Snare. Tiers: Wicker, Iron, Silver, plus Night, Water and Fae snares with bonuses in their niche.
- AI tiers: wild (random weighted), trainer (prefers effective moves), Keeper/league (type-aware switching, item use).
- Battle presentation: a 3D diorama of the zone's terrain, sprite billboards, camera swoops, per-move particles and screen shake, weather overlay.

### Around battles

- Sanctuaries: healing (wildlife rescue meets Pokécentre).
- Corner Shop: the mart.
- Ark: creature storage boxes.
- About 80 items: tea, flasks and tonics (healing), hedgerow fruit (held berries), evolution stones (Whitby Jet, Connemara Marble and others), held items, key items.
- Field abilities from badges and story: Hedge-lay (cut), Brook-wade (swim), Fell-scramble (climb), Lantern (light), Bog-cross, and late-game flight.
- Creaturepedia: seen and caught, habitat shown on the world map, real-world fact per entry, "Did you know?" pop-up on first catch.
- Saves: autosave plus 3 manual slots.

## 5. Story and progression

Professor Elspeth Garside of the Macclesfield Forest Field Station gives the player a starter and asks them to record the strange disturbances at old places across the isles. Standing stones hum; creatures behave oddly.

Villains: the Enclosure, a slick heritage and land company led by Lord Ambrose Vane. They plan to bind the ten legends into Wardstones and own the old magic, fencing off the wild (a light allegory about enclosure and the right to roam). Grunts are Wardens; there are four admins, one per nation (England, Wales, Ireland and Northern Ireland together, Scotland). Played for comedy and pomposity rather than menace.

Rival: Kit, the player's best mate from Macclesfield. Cheerful, competitive, chasing online fame. Tempted by the Enclosure midway, comes round.

Endgame: the Moot at Stonehenge (Four Winds elite plus Champion), then the London finale at the Enclosure's tower. Post-game: remaining legendaries, rematches, Orkney and Isle of Man secrets, dex completion.

Each nation's arc includes a legend moment (Nessie at Loch Ness, the Ddraig in Snowdonia, the Morrígan at Newgrange, and so on). The legend becomes catchable after its chapter.

Non-gym towns carry side quests, lore, shops. Target main story length: 20 to 30 hours.

## 6. Technical architecture

Godot 4 (latest stable 4.x), GDScript, Compatibility renderer (OpenGL 3.3) for Kepler support.

```
wildlore/
  project.godot
  core/        autoloads: GameState, SaveSystem, EventBus, Settings, Audio, SceneRouter
  data/        JSON: creatures/, moves/, items/, types.json, trainers/, zones/, dialogue/, quests/
  data_api/    loaders and typed accessors (Dex, MoveDB, ItemDB, TypeChart)
  battle/      pure logic engine: BattleState, DamageCalc, AI, Weather, StatusFX
  battle_ui/   3D diorama scene and menus driving the engine through signals
  world/       player controller, zone loader, camera rig, NPCs, warps, encounters
  zones/       one .tscn per town, route, dungeon
  ui/          menus, party, bag, Creaturepedia, world map, dialogue, shop
  story/       flags, scripted event DSL, quests
  art/         generated sprites, CC0 3D kits, shaders
  tools/       spritegen, data validator, zone builder, headless playtest bot
  tests/       GUT unit tests
```

Key decisions:

- Battle engine is pure, deterministic logic with an injected seeded RNG and no scene dependencies. It emits events; `battle_ui` only renders them. Fully unit-tested headlessly.
- Data-driven content. A validator checks every cross-reference (move ids, evolution targets, warp targets, story flags, sprite ids, encounter tables) and CI fails on any break.
- Zones use a 3D GridMap tile kit (grass, path, water, cliff, drystone wall, hedge, buildings). Movement is grid-based with smooth interpolation.
- HD-2D look: perspective camera at about 35 degrees with narrow FOV, Sprite3D billboards with nearest filtering, directional sun with shadows, glow, tilt-shift depth-of-field shader, fog cards, day/night colour grading. Quality presets Low, Medium, High; Medium must hold 60fps on the iMac.
- Audio: CC0 and synthesised SFX; folk-flavoured CC0/CC-BY music with credits.
- Saves: versioned JSON in `user://`, with migration functions per version bump.

## 7. Error handling

- Data load: validator errors are fatal in CI and in debug builds; release builds log and substitute a safe placeholder (missing sprite shows a "?" sprite, missing move is skipped) rather than crash.
- Saves: written to a temp file then renamed; the previous save kept as `.bak`; a corrupt save falls back to `.bak` with a message.
- Soft-lock guards: whiteout always returns to the last Sanctuary (or Macclesfield if none visited); a wiped party cannot trigger encounters; scripted battles always emit an end event.

## 8. Testing

- GUT unit tests for battle engine, type chart, damage, catching, evolution, saves and migrations, data API.
- Data validator run on every push.
- Headless playtest bot: walks scripted routes, fights battles, asserts invariants each frame (player never inside a wall, no soft-lock, save/load round-trips).
- Seeded fuzzer: random input monkey across random zones, run nightly in CI.
- Manual: screenshots shared at each milestone; performance check on the iMac.

## 9. Project management and delivery

Repo: `orchardroot/wildlore` (private to start). GitHub Milestones and Issues, labels (battle, world, data, art, story, audio, bug), a GitHub Project board. Feature branches and PRs merged on green CI. Commit small and push often.

CI (GitHub Actions, Linux runners): validator, GUT, playtest bot on every push; nightly fuzz; on `v*` tags, export Mac (universal .app, unsigned) and Windows (.exe), zip, attach to a GitHub Release.

Subagent strategy: Opus for design, architecture, battle engine, rendering, saves, reviews. Sonnet for zones, UI, wiring. Haiku for schema-bound data batches (creatures, moves, dex facts, dialogue, trainers). At most 2 to 3 agents in parallel (4-core Mac).

### Milestones

- M3 South and Ireland (v0.3): about 25 towns, 130 creatures, 7 badges.

Each milestone after M0 gets its own implementation plan. Screenshots go to the user at each milestone.
